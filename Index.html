Origin Airport Matters More in Timely Departure, Data Shows

Flight departure delays are more than just an airline issue, as a 150,000-flight dataset covering 2023-2025 points to another possibility: airport congestion.

By Sherlyn Petricia P · 20.09.2026

150,000 Flights analysed
2023–25 Period covered
0–100 Congestion index

Flight departure delays are more than just an airline issue, as a 150,000-flight dataset covering 2023-2025 points to another possibility: airport congestion.

A strong relationship between congestion in the origin airport and duration of delay in flight departure is shown in the dataset. As the congestion index in the origin airport increases, the average departure delays also increase.

Congestion itself could be linked to flight volumes, runway availability, aircraft movements, scheduling patterns or other operational constraints.

The dataset assigns each flight an origin-airport congestion index on a scale from zero to 100. When flights are grouped according to that measure, those departing from airports in the highest congestion band record much longer average delays than flights from airports in the lowest band.

THE MAIN FINDING

Departure delays rise sharply with airport congestion

As the origin-airport congestion index rises, the average departure delay also increases.

The gap becomes wider as congestion rises. Flights in the highest congestion band record substantially longer average delays than flights in the lowest band.

AIRPORT LEVEL

The pattern is broader than one major airport

The airport-level data adds another layer to the pattern. Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad — among the most heavily represented origins in the dataset — all have congestion scores of about 77 to 78 and average departure delays of roughly 46 minutes.

Smaller airports show a much lower congestion range and shorter average delays. For example, flights from Udaipur, Rajkot and Goa have congestion scores around 49 to 50 and average delays of about 25 to 26 minutes.

The dataset shows a broader divide between flights leaving more congested airports and those leaving less congested ones. There is a slight difference in the duration of delay between major airports.

TIMING MATTERS

Delays are higher during peak operating hours

The data also shows that delays increase during hours identified as “peak operating hours”.

This suggests that the concentration of flights at particular times may correspond with airport congestion. The dataset does not establish why peak-hour delays are higher, but the pattern points to timing as another factor worth investigating.

WEATHER

Weather has a smaller relationship with delay than congestion

Weather is associated with longer delays in some conditions, but the difference is smaller than the relationship between congestion and departure delays in this dataset.

Flights during heavier rain have longer average delays than those under clearer conditions, but the weather-related difference is smaller than the relationship between congestion and delays.

WHY IT MATTERS

The delay may begin before the aircraft leaves the runway

For passengers, the issue is straightforward: a delay begins before an aircraft leaves the runway.

If the origin airport is operating close to its limits, the pressure can appear first on the departure board, with travellers left waiting before their journey has even begun.

Longer waits can also have knock-on effects for connecting flights and onward travel.

THE SCALE

42 crore

Domestic and international passenger journeys handled by Indian airports in FY2025-26

In FY2025-26, Indian airports handled about 33.94 crore domestic passenger journeys and 8.06 crore international passenger journeys, for a combined 42.0 crore, according to the Airports Authority of India.

THE REPORTING QUESTION

If congestion is linked to longer departure delays, what is creating the bottleneck — runway capacity, gates, aircraft movements or flight scheduling?

What the data shows

The dataset shows a strong relationship between origin-airport congestion and departure-delay duration. Delays are also higher during peak operating hours, while weather shows a smaller relationship with delay than congestion in this dataset.

What the data does not prove

The analysis identifies relationships in the dataset but does not establish that airport congestion causes delays. It also cannot identify whether runways, gates, scheduling, aircraft movements or another operational constraint is responsible for the relationship.

ABOUT THE DATA

Methodology and limitations

The analysis uses a 150,000-flight dataset covering 2023-2025. Flights are grouped by the origin-airport congestion index, and average departure delays are compared across congestion bands, airport groups, peak operating periods and weather conditions.

The results should be read as patterns in the dataset rather than proof of causation.

Data source: India flight-delay dataset, 2023–2025.
